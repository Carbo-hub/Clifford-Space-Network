\begin{abstract}
Modern deep learning architectures predominantly map representations to unconstrained Euclidean coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^d$, neglecting coordinate-free geometric invariants and non-commutative compositionality. While Transformer self-attention achieves high expressive capacity, it incurs an irreducible quadratic computational complexity $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ and an exploding KV-cache memory footprint. Conversely, sub-quadratic State Space Models (SSMs) enforce strictly commutative diagonal transitions ($A_t A_{t-1} = A_{t-1} A_t$), leading to state degeneracy when capturing hierarchical order permutations and non-commutative dependencies.

This work introduces \textbf{Clifford Space Networks (CSN)}, an analytical geometric framework founded on Clifford multivector algebras $\Cl(p, q)$ and continuous \textbf{non-abelian} Lie rotor groups $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$. Unlike existing Clifford neural networks designed primarily for static PDE surrogates and spatial equivariance, CSN formulates recurrent state transitions as isometric Lie group rotations via the rational Cayley transform, executing in strictly sub-quadratic time through an $\mathcal{O}(L \log L)$ parallel associative scan and $\mathcal{O}(1)$ autoregressive generation memory.

\textbf{As a v1 feasibility study}, this paper validates the core mathematical operator across compact sequence and spatial perception tasks, clearly identifying both operating advantages and specific empirical boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Sequence Feasibility (CSN-LM)}: Under low-parameter capacity constraints on the Tiny Shakespeare character benchmark, CSN-LM $\Cl(8, 0)$ reaches \textbf{50.50\% Top-1 Accuracy} (PPL 5.46) with \textbf{158,164 parameters}, matching the accuracy of a 1.90M parameter Scaled Transformer (\textbf{19.7$\times$ parameter reduction}) and demonstrating up to \textbf{1.54$\times$ faster test-time token generation} at long contexts ($L=2048$) with constant $\mathcal{O}(1)$ memory ($\sim 21$ MB). Conversely, standard Transformers maintain higher raw parallel training token throughput due to vendor-optimized BLAS GEMMs, and diagonal SSMs (Mamba) exhibit lower per-step recurrence arithmetic ($d \cdot d_{\mathrm{state}}$ vs. $N D^2$).
    \item \textbf{Spatial Feasibility (CSN-V3)}: When trained exclusively from scratch on limited domain data (MOT16, 5.3k frames), CSN-V3 achieves \textbf{70.0\% pedestrian recall} and \textbf{50.19 F1 per million parameters} at 1.29M parameters ($1.98\times$ fewer than YOLO26n) with fused CUDA scan kernels operating at real-time speeds (54.98 ms / 18.2 FPS on a GTX 1660 Ti). Conversely, the fully pre-trained YOLO26n baseline (MS COCO 118k images) achieves higher overall F1 (73.60\% vs. 65.06\%), lower forward latency (46.78 ms), and lower peak VRAM (80.7 MB vs. 107.6 MB), reflecting the maturity of 2D convolutional implementations.
\end{enumerate}
Finally, we position CSN primarily as an \textbf{analytical solver for formal logic graphs and physical Hamiltonian systems}. We define the immediate \textbf{v2 roadmap} focused strictly on empirical scaling across standard corpora (BabyLM, TinyStories, ImageNet) and new modalities, while mapping out long-term theoretical horizons including \textbf{Port-Hamiltonian Interconnections (PHS)} to resolve the neuron interconnect bottleneck, and \textbf{orbifold quotient geometries} for critical singularities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The modeling of dynamic temporal processes, non-commutative symbolic structures, and continuous physical systems remains constrained by foundational algebraic trade-offs across modern neural architectures.

\subsection{The Non-Abelian Dilemma in Sequence Modeling}

In sequence modeling and natural language processing, the Transformer architecture~\cite{vaswani2017attention} relies on unconstrained pairwise dot-product attention:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V
\end{equation}
Although empirically dominant at scale, self-attention incurs an irreducible quadratic computational bottleneck $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ and an exploding Key-Value ($KV$) cache memory footprint during autoregressive generation.

To achieve linear computation, State Space Models (SSMs) such as S4, S5, and Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba} introduce input-dependent linear recurrences. However, to enable the fast parallel prefix scan (Blelloch scan) in $\mathcal{O}(L \log L)$, SSMs enforce that the state transition matrix $A_t$ is strictly \textbf{diagonal}:
\begin{equation}
    h_t = A_t h_{t-1} + B_t x_t, \quad A_t = \mathrm{diag}(a_{t,1}, \dots, a_{t,N})
\end{equation}
Consequently, consecutive state transitions are strictly \textbf{commutative} ($A_t A_{t-1} = A_{t-1} A_t$). In contrast, grammatical syntax, algorithmic reasoning, and formal logic are fundamentally \textbf{non-commutative}; the semantic state depends critically on token order and non-trivial permutations ($A \circ B \neq B \circ A$). To capture non-commutative hierarchies, commutative SSMs must significantly inflate their hidden state dimensions.

Similarly, Closed-form Continuous-time (CfC) networks~\cite{hasani2022closed} model dynamical systems via continuous ODEs with explicit decay:
\begin{equation}
    h_t = \sigma(-f(x_t) \Delta t) \odot \tanh(W_1 x_t) + (1 - \sigma(-f(x_t) \Delta t)) \odot \tanh(W_2 x_t)
\end{equation}
While continuous, CfC couples inputs and recurrent states inside non-linear saturating activations ($\tanh$), which precludes formulation as an associative matrix scan and restricts training to sequential $\mathcal{O}(L)$ loops.

\subsection{Distinction from Prior Clifford Neural Networks}

The integration of Clifford (geometric) algebras into machine learning has garnered significant attention, particularly through Clifford Neural Layers for PDE modeling~\cite{brandstetter2022clifford}, Geometric Clifford Algebra Networks (GCANs)~\cite{ruhe2023geometric}, Clifford Group Equivariant Networks (CGENN)~\cite{ruhe2023clifford}, and Geometric Algebra Transformers (GATr)~\cite{brehmer2023geometric}.

However, a fundamental architectural distinction separates CSN from prior literature:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Target Domain}: Prior Clifford networks focus almost exclusively on static spatial symmetry groups ($O(n), E(n)$) or surrogate modelling for continuous Partial Differential Equations (e.g., fluid dynamics, weather forecasting). In contrast, CSN targets \textbf{sub-quadratic dynamic sequence modeling and state space transitions}.
    \item \textbf{Transition Mechanics}: While previous works apply static geometric products of multivector weights ($W \star x$), CSN formulates recurrent state updates via the \textbf{rational Cayley transform} on Lie algebras $\mathfrak{so}(n) \to \mathrm{Spin}(n)$.
    \item \textbf{Associative Scans}: Prior multivector architectures do not formulate causal temporal recurrences or require $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ attention (as in GATr). CSN derives an exact affine closed form that admits a \textbf{parallel prefix scan in $\mathcal{O}(L \log L)$} and register-level fused GPU kernels.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Primary Orientation: Analytical Graph and Physical Solvers}

While standard deep learning architectures approximate tasks through statistical pattern matching on flat Euclidean tensors, Clifford Space Networks were conceived as an \textbf{analytical engine}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Analytical Logic Graph Solvers}: In formal logic, causal dependency networks, and knowledge graphs, relations are non-commutative and structured. By mapping entities to multivectors and relations to Spinor Lie rotors $R_{ij} \in \mathrm{Spin}(n)$, logical inference along graph paths reduces to exact algebraic rotor composition without geometric distortion.
    \item \textbf{Physical System Modeling and Gauge Invariants}: Physical conservation laws (Hamiltonian mechanics, Maxwell's electrodynamics $F = E + I c B$, and gauge field theories) are natively expressed via differential forms and bivectors. CSN operates directly on the Lie group $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ with strict algebraic norm preservation, providing an inductive bias for physical dynamical systems.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Architectural Scope: v1 Feasibility and Operating Boundaries}

This work constitutes a \textbf{Version 1 (v1) architectural feasibility study}. The empirical benchmarks presented herein validate that non-abelian Lie rotor scans can be executed with exact isometry, sub-quadratic training, and constant inference memory.

We explicitly recognize the empirical boundaries of this first iteration:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Established Transformer Scaling}: While CSN demonstrates superior parameter efficiency under compact capacity constraints, standard Transformers benefit from established scaling laws across hundreds of billions of tokens, where self-attention patterns and dense Tensor Core operations remain dominant.
    \item \textbf{Arithmetic Overhead}: CSN computes per-channel matrix Cayley inversions ($N D^2$), incurring higher per-token arithmetic cost than diagonal SSMs or standard feedforward projections.
    \item \textbf{Dataset Scope}: The v1 evaluations focus on controlled compact datasets (Tiny Shakespeare, MOT16). Phase 2 (v2) will systematically benchmark against large-scale standardized corpora (BabyLM 10M/100M, TinyStories, ImageNet-1k).
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Foundations: Clifford Algebras and Lie Rotors}

\subsection{Clifford Algebra $\Cl(p, q)$}

Let $V = \mathbb{R}^{p+q}$ be an $n$-dimensional real vector space equipped with a quadratic form $Q$ of signature $(p, q)$ where $n = p + q$. The Clifford algebra $\Cl(p, q)$ is generated by $V$ subject to:
\begin{equation}
    v^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1, \quad \forall v \in V
\end{equation}
Given an orthonormal basis $\{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$, the generators satisfy:
\begin{equation}
    e_i e_j + e_j e_i = 2 \eta_{ij} \mathbf{1}, \quad \eta_{ij} = \mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{+1, \dots, +1}_{p}, \underbrace{-1, \dots, -1}_{q})
\end{equation}
A multivector $A \in \Cl(p, q)$ admits a canonical grade decomposition:
\begin{equation}
    A = \langle A \rangle_0 + \langle A \rangle_1 + \langle A \rangle_2 + \dots + \langle A \rangle_n = \sum_{k=0}^n \langle A \rangle_k
\end{equation}
where $\langle A \rangle_k \in \bigwedge^k V$ corresponds to scalars ($k=0$), vectors ($k=1$), bivectors ($k=2$), and pseudo-scalars ($k=n$).

\subsection{Spinor Lie Group and the Rational Cayley Transform}

The even sub-algebra $\Cl^+(p, q) = \bigoplus_{k \text{ even}} \bigwedge^k V$ defines the Spinor group:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Spin}(p, q) = \{ R \in \Cl^+(p, q) \mid R \tilde{R} = 1, \; \forall v \in V: R v \tilde{R} \in V \}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{R}$ is the reversion anti-automorphism. In the Euclidean signature $\Cl(n, 0)$, $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ doubly covers $\mathrm{SO}(n)$:
\begin{equation}
    1 \to \mathbb{Z}_2 \to \mathrm{Spin}(n) \xrightarrow{\rho} \mathrm{SO}(n) \to 1, \quad \rho(R)(v) = R v \tilde{R}
\end{equation}
The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{spin}(n) \cong \mathfrak{so}(n)$ consists of pure bivectors $\langle A \rangle_2 = \sum_{i < j} b_{ij} e_i \wedge e_j$.

To map bivectors $\Omega \in \mathfrak{so}(n)$ into group rotations without numerical truncation, CSN applies the \textbf{rational Cayley transform}:
\begin{equation}
    R = \mathcal{C}(\Omega) = (I + \Omega)(I - \Omega)^{-1} \in \mathrm{Spin}(n)
\end{equation}
The Cayley transform is strictly isometric:
\begin{equation}
    \|R v\| = \|v\|, \quad \forall v \in \mathbb{R}^n
\end{equation}
guaranteeing bounded spectral radius and zero gradient vanishing/explosion by geometric construction.

\subsection{Exact Clifford Closed Form vs. Heuristic CfC}

In contrast to empirical bounding functions on flat vectors~\cite{hasani2022closed}, CSN defines an \textbf{exact algebraic closed form}:
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_t = \frac{1}{2} B(x_t) \Delta t_t, \quad R_t = (I + \Omega_t)(I - \Omega_t)^{-1} \in \mathrm{Spin}(n)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    h_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t} R_t h_{t-1} + (1 - \alpha_t) S_t \equiv M_t h_{t-1} + C_t
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_t = \sigma(-\gamma(x_t) \Delta t_t) \in (0, 1)$ is continuous decay, $M_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t} R_t$, and $C_t = (1 - \alpha_t) S_t$.

Because $(M_j, C_j) \circ (M_i, C_i) = (M_j M_i, M_j C_i + C_j)$ satisfies exact mathematical associativity:
\begin{equation}
    \left( (M_k, C_k) \circ (M_j, C_j) \right) \circ (M_i, C_i) = (M_k, C_k) \circ \left( (M_j, C_j) \circ (M_i, C_i) \right)
\end{equation}
the entire sequence can be trained via the \textbf{parallel associative prefix scan} in $\mathcal{O}(L \log L)$ time.

\section{Feasibility Benchmark: Dynamic Sequence Modeling}

\subsection{Architecture of CSN-LM}

In CSN-LM, input token sequences $x \in \{1, \dots, V\}^L$ are projected into embedding dimension $\mathbb{R}^{d}$. Each Clifford recurrent layer computes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Input State Injection}: $S_t = W_{\text{in}} x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D \times D}$ for $N$ Clifford neurons and dimension $D$.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Adaptive Pacing}: $\Delta t_t = \mathrm{softplus}(W_{\Delta t} x_t) \cdot \Delta t_{\mathrm{base}}$.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Conductance Decay}: $\alpha_t = \sigma(-\mathrm{softplus}(W_\gamma x_t) \Delta t_t) \in (0, 1)$.
    \item \textbf{Non-Abelian Generator}: $\Omega_t = \frac{1}{2} \Delta t_t \sum_{i < j} b_{ij}(x_t) (E_{ij} - E_{ji}) \in \mathfrak{so}(D)$.
    \item \textbf{Rational Cayley Rotor}: $R_t = (I + \Omega_t)(I - \Omega_t)^{-1} \in \mathrm{Spin}(D)$.
    \item \textbf{Affine Recurrence}: $h_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t} R_t h_{t-1} + (1 - \alpha_t) S_t$.
\end{enumerate}

To decode logits without unconstrained Euclidean projections, CSN-LM employs a \textbf{Hyperbolic Cosine Centroid Head}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{logit}_c = s \cdot \cos\left(d_g(h_t, \mathcal{E}_c)\right) + b_c = s \cdot \frac{\langle h_t, \mathcal{E}_c \rangle}{\|h_t\| \, \|\mathcal{E}_c\|} + b_c
\end{equation}

Similarly, diagonal state space models (Mamba S6) feature smaller baseline parameter counts (72k) and simpler element-wise operations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Roadmap and Future Architectural Horizons}

\subsection{Phase 2 (v2) Agenda: Empirical Scaling and Benchmark Generalization}

Rather than coupling architectural complexity prematurely, the concrete scope of the upcoming \textbf{Version 2 (v2)} iteration is defined strictly around systematic empirical scaling:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Standardized Sequence Benchmarks}: Evaluating CSN against contemporary LLaMA-style architectures (RoPE + RMSNorm + SwiGLU) and Mamba SSMs across the standardized \textbf{BabyLM Challenge (10M / 100M)} and \textbf{TinyStories} datasets on high-throughput NVIDIA RTX 4090 hardware.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Scaling to ImageNet-1k}: Progressing from MOT16 pedestrian detection to full-scale hierarchical visual classification on ImageNet-1k to evaluate how dual-grade Clifford backbones scale relative to Vision Transformers (ViT) and modern ConvNets.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Task Generalization}: Evaluating CSN across continuous time-series forecasting and physical trajectory prediction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Long-Term Horizon: Port-Hamiltonian Interconnections (PHS)}

A primary structural finding from this v1 study is the \textbf{interconnect bottleneck}: each Clifford neuron possesses high internal geometric dimensionality (up to 256 multivector degrees of freedom), yet inter-neuron communication is mediated by standard flat Euclidean projection matrices $W$. 

To address this limitation in future major iterations, a principled long-term research direction is the formulation of inter-neuron coupling via \textbf{Port-Hamiltonian Systems (PHS)}~\cite{vanderschaft2014port}:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{pmatrix} \dot{x} \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} J(x) - R(x) & G(x) \\ -G^T(x) & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \nabla H(x) \\ u \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $H(x) \ge 0$ represents the internal Hamiltonian energy of multivector states, $J(x) = -J^T(x)$ is a skew-symmetric Dirac structure preserving rotational energy during inter-neuron exchange, $R(x) \succeq 0$ provides dissipation ensuring Lyapunov passivity ($\dot{H} \le y^T u$), and $(u, y)$ are port variables. Formulating network depth as power-conserving Dirac interconnections provides an analytical foundation to eliminate the interconnect bottleneck.

\subsection{Fundamental Theoretical Frontier: Orbifold Geometries for Critical Singularities}

As a distinct, long-term theoretical frontier, future foundational work will investigate extending state manifolds from smooth Riemannian spaces to \textbf{orbifold quotients} $\mathcal{O} = \mathcal{Cl}(p, q) / \Gamma$, where $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{Spin}(n)$ is a discrete symmetry subgroup.

Smooth manifolds are locally Euclidean and inherently lack singular points. Consequently, when neural models encounter critical bifurcations, phase transitions, or discrete logical branch points, smooth parameterizations often exhibit gradient instability. In contrast, orbifolds naturally accommodate cone-point singularities and fold redundant symmetric state volumes into dense quotient fundamental domains. Extending Clifford multivectors to orbifold structures constitutes a rich, standalone mathematical research program to amplify parameter density and stabilize critical edge cases.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper has presented Version 1 of Clifford Space Networks (CSN), introducing continuous non-abelian Lie rotor scans as an analytically grounded, sub-quadratic alternative to unconstrained Euclidean networks. 

By grounding state transitions in the rational Cayley transform on $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ and establishing strict algebraic isometry, CSN demonstrates remarkable sample efficiency and constant inference memory on compact feasibility benchmarks. While Version 2 focuses squarely on empirical scaling across standard corpora (BabyLM, TinyStories, ImageNet), this work establishes a rigorous mathematical foundation and charts clear long-term horizons toward Port-Hamiltonian interconnections and orbifold geometries.
